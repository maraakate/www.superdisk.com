Imation and Grand Prix Racing Team Drive The Fast Lane With SuperDisk, Travan Technologies

Imation and Leading European Racing Team, Jordan Grand Prix, Enter Into a Technical Partnership for 1999 Racing Season

OAKDALE, Minn. --- (May 11, 1999) Combining state-of-the-art data storage and information management technology with the rigors and demands of high-tech auto racing, Imation Corp. (NYSE:IMN) today announced that it has entered into a technical partnership with British racing team Jordan Grand Prix for the 1999 Formula One season.

The partnership involves Jordans use of Imation data storage products for the capture, storage and management of the immense amount of data the team manages during every phase of the Formula One season, including prototype development, aerodynamics testing, trial runs and actual racing.

"The connection between modern IT technology and auto racing isnt that evident at first sight," explained Neill Ewen, marketing operations manager for Imation Desktop/Mobile products in Europe. "But computers and data storage technology play an absolutely vital role in nearly all areas of Formula One."

"Most people think that Formula One or motor racing in general just has to do with engines, tires, gears, chassis tuning and so on," explained Eddie Jordan, owner and chief executive, Jordan Grand Prix. "Very few realize how much goes on behind the scenes, just how dependent the sport is on computer-aided technology. From the smallest part to the complete chassis, most components in todays Formula One racing cars are designed with a computer, and this means we need strong, reliable solutions for storing, reading, maintaining and updating this crucial information."

Seven years after its entry into Formula One racing, Jordan Grand Prix ranks in the top echelons of the sport. This year, Jordan will vie for the Formula One World Championship with Imations SuperDisk and Travan technologies playing a critical, behind-the-scenes role.

"Imation's SuperDisk technology is a great time-saver and trouble-saver when we're processing or storing our data on desktop or notebook PCs," explained Aubrey Mitchel, IT manager at Jordan. "For example, a Jordan Grand Prix car generates several hundred kilobytes of telemetric data with each lap around the circuit. To collect this data, we had to use a new, 1.44 MB diskette for nearly every single lap. Now, we can store all of this data on a single SuperDisk diskette."

Jordan also collects and manages data regarding vehicle design and construction -- such as data about the aerodynamic properties of the car with the help of Jordan's own computer network and Imation's Travan minicartridges.

"When we first sought a technical partner, we considered Imations brand values - a high technology focus driven by innovation," said Ewen. "When we then combined them with our data storage values of speed, reliability and performance, Formula One became the obvious choice. Formula One is exceedingly popular within the IT industry in Europe, and our association with Jordan provides the ideal promotional platform from which to showcase our technology solutions to our target markets in an exciting way."

About Jordan Grand Prix

Jordan Grand Prix is headquartered at a four-acre site at Silverstone, England, across the road from Britain's famous motor racing circuit and home of the British Grand Prix. The team's headquarters boasts a technology package worthy of a top Formula One team, including a 40% scale wind tunnel (located five miles away in Brackley), a seven-post rig (which allows the car to run on a simulated race track) and gear box test rig.
